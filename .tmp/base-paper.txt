ABSTRACT
The Medicine Supply Chain (MSC) faces critical challenges in traceability, transparency, and counterfeit
prevention. This paper proposes a Blockchain-​based solution using Ethereum smart contracts (developed
in Solidity, deployed via Truffle, and tested on Rinkeby and localhost networks) to enhance MSC integrity. The framework introduces consumer authentication, Universal Product Code (UPC) registration,
and transaction logging to ensure end-​to-​end drug traceability. By embedding multilingual UPCs (e.g.,
Urdu, Hindi), the system improves accessibility for diverse healthcare users, who can verify medicine
authenticity via QR codes or a dedicated mobile app. The study examines scalability challenges (e.g.,
managing 100K+ UPCs in large healthcare networks) and evaluates the shifting dynamics among stakeholders (suppliers, processors, retailers). Public Blockchain implementation demonstrates potential for
decentralized, tamper-​proof record-​keeping, though trade-​offs in efficiency and adoption barriers persist.

7 CONCLUSION
In this research, we developed a Blockchain model implemented in solidity to create an Ethereum
smart contract. We utilized Truffle for development and tested the smart contracts on the ‘localhost’
and ‘rinkeby’ tenets. While other testnets are available, ‘rinkeby’ was chosen for this study. The proposed supply chain model highlights the advantages and limitations of Blockchain technology in asset
traceability compared to traditional tracking systems. Consumers can use QR codes to access detailed
information about products before they are blocked in the network, fostering consumer confidence and
potentially increasing customer loyalty and revenue. The balance of power between processors and
retailers may shift through Blockchain implementation. Future research could explore the scalability of
Blockchain systems, especially in managing a vast number of items, such as UPCs, in a large healthcare
setting. This paper aims to offer valuable insights for supply chain professionals on how blockchain
could disrupt existing supply chain models and address challenges to its implementation. Future work

regular location and temperature updates, and expanding the system to cover additional medical supplies.
